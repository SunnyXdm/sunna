Use any of your computers from the one in front of you.

Sunna is a remote desktop for machines you own. Click a computer and you’re at its screen, with its sound, your keyboard and your clipboard. Your devices talk to each other directly: no accounts, and nothing passes through a server of ours.

Set it up once

  1. Share a computer

    On the computer you want to reach, turn on sharing. You get a link, and a QR code for your phone.

  2. Add it on your other devices

    Paste the link into the app, or scan the code. The link carries that computer’s identity, and the app checks it before it sends anything.

  3. Open it

    When the computer shows Ready, click or tap it. Its desktop opens full screen, and Disconnect in the session menu brings you back.

Computers view

Every computer, as you left it

Each computer shows what was on its screen when you left, and whether you can reach it now. Gallery puts them side by side; Devices, Wall, Space and Type arrange the same computers four other ways.

The picture is taken once, as you leave a session, and stays on your device. You can blur it or turn it off.

Sharing is one switch

On Linux, open Settings and turn on Share this computer. It stays shared after you close the window, and hands you the link to paste on your other devices.

The same switch is written for the Mac and is waiting for its first test on one.

Update stage

Update a computer without going to it

The app shows which of your computers are behind, and updates one when you ask. That computer checks the release’s signature, switches version in one step, and goes back if the new one doesn’t start. It never restarts while someone is connected.

Built and tested. It starts working with the first public release.

Restart it, then sign in from here

Set up for the whole machine, a Linux host starts before anyone signs in. After a reboot you see the login screen, type your password, and land on your desktop in the same session.

Experimental. Proven with LightDM and SDDM in test machines, not yet on real hardware.

Typing that keeps up

Video takes time to encode, most of all on a computer without a GPU. So Sunna sends small changes, like the letter you just typed, as exact image tiles on a lane of their own, ahead of the video.

What runs where

Reach a Mac or a Linux computer from a Mac, a Linux computer or an Android phone. A Linux computer can share an X11 desktop, GNOME on Wayland, or a server with no screen.

Mac to Mac and Mac to Linux are in daily use. The Linux and Android apps are newer.

Get Sunna

Today you build Sunna from source, and each guide has the one script that does it. Signed downloads and a one-line installer are built and tested; they arrive with the first release.

  1. Your network

    Sunna works on any network you trust. With Tailscale, free for personal use, your computers are reachable from anywhere and closed to everyone else. Keep a host off the public internet.
